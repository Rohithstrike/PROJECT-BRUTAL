02 · Transformation target

Lean + Muscular + Athletic

The target is a physique that performs: lower waist, retained (or improved) muscle and strength, and a markedly better engine. Outcomes are framed as processes and trends — not guarantees. Ab visibility depends mostly on overall body-fat level, genetics and fat distribution, which no program can promise on a date.

Fat-loss dashboard

Target weight?

There is deliberately no fixed final bodyweight. Let waist, performance and weekly weight trend determine the target. A sensible fat-loss rate for muscle retention is roughly 0.5–1.0% of bodyweight per week (weekly average, not daily).

Why the abdomen is last to lean out

Spot reduction is not supported by evidence: crunches do not burn the fat on top of the abs. Abdominal fat falls as total body fat falls through a sustained energy deficit. Your recent gain was largely stored centrally, so expect it to leave gradually — and often last.

What we track (weekly)

  • Weekly average weight — 3–7 morning weigh-ins, same conditions
  • Waist — at navel, relaxed exhale, Sunday morning
  • Photos — front/side/back, same light & distance, every Sunday
  • Strength retention — main lifts held or rising at the same RIR
  • Energy — 1–10 daily score
  • Adherence — sessions, steps, sleep
NO CRASH DIETING
Very aggressive deficits accelerate muscle and strength loss, hurt training quality, raise hunger and rebound risk. No starvation, no dehydration tricks, no "fat burner" stimulants. If weight drops >1% per week for 2+ weeks and lifts are falling, eat more.

04 · Weekly program

The optimised split

Your original idea was close. The final split keeps your favourite body-part days but engineers the fatigue: heavy quads (Wed) are separated from the hard HYROX engine (Fri) by 48 h; the posterior-chain day (Sat) is moderate-load so it survives Friday; hard running never directly precedes heavy squats. Click any day to open the full session.

Why this split

  • Mon Push / Tue Pull — fresh after Sunday; upper days carry only easy Zone 2, so legs recover.
  • Wed Lower A — heaviest leg day, 96 h after Saturday’s legs. No running or skipping on this day.
  • Thu Shoulders + Arms — lateral/rear delts get a second weekly hit; easy running builds tolerance without leg damage.
  • Fri HYROX Foundation — the week’s hard engine day, 48 h after heavy squats, upper athletic strength + carries.
  • Sat Lower B — posterior chain + unilateral + sled at RIR 2–3 loads: a second leg exposure that builds athletic capacity without wrecking Monday.
  • Sun — recovery, walking, mobility, weekly check-in.

Are two leg days useful? Yes.

Training a muscle about twice per week is a well-supported way to distribute weekly volume with better session quality. Here:

  • Lower A = quad dominant, heaviest loading (squat, press, split squat, extensions, adductors, calves).
  • Lower B = hinge, glutes, hamstrings, lunges, soleus, tibialis, sled — the HYROX-relevant pattern.
  • Both days include knee-flexion hamstring work, so hamstrings get 2× weekly direct stimulus.
  • Leg isolation goes close to failure; leg compounds stay at RIR 2 — enough stimulus, far less systemic fatigue for running.

Estimated weekly hard sets (Phase 1 baseline)

Direct sets count 1, meaningful indirect sets count 0.5. Most major muscles sit in a roughly 8–16 set/week zone — enough to grow in a deficit without junk volume. See Muscle Coverage for every phase.

06 · Cardio engine

Build the engine, protect the legs

Mostly easy, some hard, never random. Roughly 75–85% of weekly cardio minutes are low intensity (Zone 2 / incline walking), which supports fat loss, recovery and aerobic base with minimal interference with strength. Hard efforts are concentrated on Friday (and a short Tuesday dose in Phase 3).

Weekly cardio progression

10,000 steps / day

An activity target, not a magic fat-loss number. Steps (NEAT) are a low-fatigue way to raise daily energy expenditure and help keep it stable while dieting, when people unconsciously move less.

  • Gym warm-ups, treadmill work and runs count toward the total.
  • Wed & Sat (leg days): 8–10k is fine — do not force extra walking on sore legs.
  • Sunday: 10–12k easy steps as active recovery.
  • Easy wins: 10–15 min walk after meals, calls on foot, stairs.
  • Never add 20k+ days to “compensate” — that eats into recovery.

Skipping — controlled

Great for coordination, footwork, warm-ups and short conditioning — but ground contacts load calves and Achilles on top of running and leg training.

  • Use: warm-ups Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri (2–3 min); Tue conditioning in Phase 2 (≤5 min work); optional Sunday 3–5 min easy.
  • Avoid: Wed & Sat (leg days), immediately before runs >20 min, and any day with Achilles/shin soreness.
  • Weekly cap: ~10 min (Ph 1) → ~15 min (Ph 2) → ~20 min (Ph 3), including warm-ups.
  • Low, quiet hops on a forgiving surface; stop at any sharp Achilles/shin pain.

Optional morning protocol

Two-a-days are not mandatory. A morning block is only worth it if it’s low-fatigue and doesn’t steal quality from the main session.

Useful mornings

  • 20–40 min easy walk (steps + mood), any day
  • 20–30 min Zone 2 on Mon, Tue, Thu if total weekly load feels fine
  • 10–15 min mobility flow (hips, T-spine, ankles)
  • 5–8 min core-stability primer (dead bug, side plank) on non-core days
  • Sunday: easy walk + mobility

Not useful

  • Extra lifting sessions (adds fatigue, not results)
  • Hard intervals before Wed Lower A or Sat Lower B
  • Morning runs on Fri (Friday already contains the hard engine)
  • Any morning block on a YELLOW/RED readiness day
  • Fasted hard cardio for “more fat burn” — total daily balance is what counts
07 · Core / abs

Abs are built — then revealed

Core is trained 4× per week (Mon, Wed, Sat direct + Fri loaded carries), never hard every day. Abs are muscles: they get load, progression and recovery like any other. But visible abs are determined mainly by body-fat reduction — endless crunches cannot remove the fat covering them.

Core schedule by phase

Progress abs like a muscle

  1. Reps — reach the top of the range at the RIR target.
  2. Range / lever — knees → straight legs; kneeling → longer rollout.
  3. Load — heavier cable stack, DB between feet, plate on chest.
  4. Tempo / pause — 2 s squeeze, 3 s eccentric.

Change one variable at a time. Planks: once 60 s is easy, make it harder (RKC tension, long lever, weighted) rather than holding 4 minutes.

Hip-flexion-aware technique

  • Leg raises train abs only if the pelvis curls up (posterior tilt). Swinging legs = mostly hip flexors.
  • Cable crunch: flex the ribcage toward the pelvis; hips stay fixed — don’t sit back and pull with arms.
  • Ab wheel: ribs down, glutes squeezed; stop the range before the lower back sags.
  • Dead bug: lower back stays gently pressed down; exhale fully on each rep.
  • Any lower-back pain during core work → regress the lever, don’t push through.
08 · HYROX foundation

Capacity now. Specificity later.

A HYROX race is 8 × 1 km runs, each followed by a functional station. This block is not race-specific: it builds aerobic base, running tolerance, station technique and compromised-running ability while the main goal stays physique + strength. Race-specific prep (full distances, race weights, pacing) starts in the next block once a race date is chosen.

StationCapacity builtWhere in programGym alternative (not identical)

Substitutes train related capacities but are not physiologically identical — e.g. a treadmill “sled push” lacks the ground friction profile of a real sled; straight-arm pulldowns miss the continuous hip-hinge rhythm of the SkiErg. Use the real equipment whenever available.

Friday HYROX block — all 13 weeks

Saturday sled / engine progression

Compromised running

Running straight after stations teaches you to find rhythm on heavy legs. Introduced in Week 6 at controlled effort; progressed in Weeks 9–11.

Transitions

Practise starting the next station within 10–15 s: equipment pre-set, no phone checks. Transition speed is free time.

Pacing rule

Every simulation is run at a pace you could repeat. If your last round is the slowest by a lot, you started too hard — log it and adjust.

09 · Progression system

How to progress

Progressive overload is the non-negotiable driver: more reps, more load, or better quality at the same effort — tracked, not guessed. In a calorie deficit, maintaining strength on compounds is already a win; accessories should still creep up.

Heavy · 3–8 reps
STRENGTH

Big compounds (bench, squat, RDL, trap-bar, pull-up, OHP). Low reps let you use high loads with crisp technique; stopping at RIR 2 keeps fatigue manageable for concurrent cardio. Rest 2.5–3.5 min.

Moderate · 6–12 reps
HYPERTROPHY

Secondary compounds and heavier isolation (incline DB, rows, leg press, curls). Best balance of load, volume and time-efficiency. RIR 1–2, rest 1.5–2 min.

High · 12–25+ reps
ENDURANCE / PUMP

Small muscles and joint-friendly isolation (lateral raises, rear delts, calves, flyes, abs). Hypertrophy occurs across wide rep ranges when sets are taken close to failure; higher reps here spare joints. RIR 0–1, rest 45–75 s.

RIR — reps in reserve

0No rep left (isolation only, last set)
1One rep left — isolation default
2Two left — compound default
3Three left — Week 1 / technique
4Deload / warm-up quality

Log RIR honestly. If the last rep slows but stays technically identical, you’re near RIR 1. If form breaks, the set ended one rep earlier.

Rules by exercise type

Intensity techniques — clearly labelled

  • SUPERSET — antagonist or non-competing pairs to save time (e.g. triceps + lateral raise). Rest after B.
  • DROP SET — final set only, isolation only: hit RIR 0–1, drop ~25%, go to RIR 1.
  • MYO-REPS — final set only: activation set to RIR 1, then 3 mini-sets of 3–5 reps with ~15 s rests.
  • REST-PAUSE — final set: 15 s pause, 2 mini-sets.
  • Never on squats, deadlifts, bench, OHP, pull-ups or rows with the spine loaded.
  • Introduced from Week 3; removed in Weeks 1, 8, 13.

When to back off

  • Reps drop on a compound for 2 sessions in a row at the same load → reduce 5–10% and rebuild.
  • Joint pain (not muscle burn) on a movement → swap to the listed substitute.
  • 2+ YELLOW readiness days in a week → cut 1 set per exercise for the rest of the week.
  • Sleep <6 h → skip intensity techniques, keep the main lifts.
  • Weekly weight loss >1% for 2+ weeks with falling performance → raise calories slightly.

11 · Recovery

Train hard. Recover harder.

Adaptation happens between sessions. In a calorie deficit with 6 training days, recovery is the limiting factor — manage it deliberately.

Daily readiness check

Answer the questions to get today’s call.

GREEN — train normally

  • Slept ≥7 h, felt normal
  • Soreness mild
  • Warm-up weights feel normal
  • Resting HR within ~5 bpm of your normal

Run the session as written.

YELLOW — reduce slightly

  • Short/poor sleep, low motivation
  • Noticeable soreness
  • Warm-ups feel heavy
  • Resting HR ~5–10 bpm high

−1 set per exercise, +1 RIR, skip techniques, cardio → Zone 2 only.

RED — recovery / rest

  • Illness signs, unusual fatigue
  • Pain that changes movement
  • Performance clearly down 2+ sessions
  • Resting HR >10 bpm high

20–40 min easy walk + mobility, or full rest. Seek medical advice for concerning symptoms.

Pillars

  • Sleep: 7.5–9 h target, consistent wake time. Poor sleep during a deficit increases muscle loss risk and hunger.
  • Rest between sessions: same muscle trained hard ≥48 h apart (Wed quads ↔ Sat posterior; Mon push ↔ Thu shoulders).
  • Sunday: full recovery day — walking, mobility, meal prep, check-in.
  • Deloads: Week 8 (planned), Week 13 (assessment). Reactive trim any week via readiness.
  • Soreness: mild soreness is normal; train through with lighter warm-ups. Soreness that alters technique = reduce load that day.
  • Performance monitoring: if the logbook trend stalls for 2 weeks on most lifts, fatigue — not laziness — is the usual cause.

Sunday mobility flow (15–20 min)

    No pain-seeking stretches. Mobility work is about moving well, not forcing range.

    Warm-up standard (5–10 min)

    1. General: 4–5 min incline walk / bike / row / easy skipping (where allowed)
    2. Specific: joints & activation for the day’s muscles
    3. Ramp sets for first lift: ~50% × 6–8 → 70% × 3–5 → 85% × 1–2

    Cool-down (3–5 min)

    Easy walk until breathing settles, then 2–3 relaxed stretches for the trained area. Optional but helps you downshift after intense sessions.

    12 · Nutrition fundamentals

    Fuel the deficit, protect the muscle

    This is a training program, not a diet plan. Fat loss is driven by a sustained, modest calorie deficit; muscle retention by adequate protein + hard training + sleep. Your exact calorie needs are unknown — estimate, then calibrate with 2–3 weeks of real data.

    Deficit
    ~300–500kcal/day

    Aim for ~0.5–1% BW/week

    Protein
    1.6–2.2g/kg/day

    ≈ 140–195 g at 89 kg, over 3–5 meals

    Fibre
    30–40g/day

    Vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains

    Hydration
    Paleurine

    ~2.5–3.5 L + sweat losses; never dehydrate

    Maintenance estimator

    Mifflin–St Jeor BMR × activity factor. A starting estimate only (error of ±10% or more is common).

    Calibrate with real data

    1. Eat at the estimated target consistently for 2–3 weeks (Weeks 1–3).
    2. Compare weekly average weight, not single days.
    3. Losing <0.5%/wk and waist unchanged → reduce ~150–200 kcal/day or add ~2–3k daily steps.
    4. Losing 0.5–1%/wk, strength stable → change nothing.
    5. Losing >1%/wk with falling performance or poor sleep → add ~100–200 kcal/day (mostly carbs around training).
    6. Re-check every 2 weeks. Expect water-weight noise (salt, carbs, stress, sleep).

    Meal structure

    • 3–4 meals, each with a protein anchor (~30–50 g)
    • Carbs concentrated before/after training
    • Consistent timing beats perfect timing

    Food quality

    • Mostly minimally processed foods
    • Lean meats, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, rice, oats, potatoes, fruit, vegetables
    • Planned flexible foods (~10–20%) help adherence

    Adherence levers

    • Alcohol: limit — calories + sleep disruption
    • Holidays (Wk 12): maintenance-level eating is fine for a few days
    • Caffeine: moderate; avoid late-day use; no “fat burners”
